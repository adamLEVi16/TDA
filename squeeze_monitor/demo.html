Squeeze early-warning monitor

A risk tool for a consumer/retail short book: crowded short interest (FINRA, published data only) × abnormal public attention → step-aside flag. Free data, fully reproducible. It flags danger; it does not predict direction. Universe and episodes below are tradable consumer/retail names — GME and AMC are excluded throughout, including from the significance test.

Episode 1 — Carvana, November 2023

CVNA is also the episode that exposed a mechanical flaw in the simpler days-to-cover crowding test: DTC falls as volume explodes mid-squeeze, so it can read "safe" exactly when risk peaks. The volume-independent crowding measure used here (short-interest level vs. its own trailing history) doesn't have that failure mode — though on CVNA specifically its own 2022 collapse-era extremes keep the percentile moderate even in a real squeeze, which is why this episode is shown via the weekly rule (the one behind the p = 0.032 population result) rather than the finer daily view. Stated, not hidden.

Honest limitations (on the sheet, not in a footnote)

  • Misses squeezes with no attention spike — e.g. CVNA's earlier July 2023 move (roughly +50% in two weeks) was not flagged, a different episode from the Nov 2023 one shown above that was — or that lack SI history (BYND 2019, IPO'd too recently for a trailing baseline).
  • Delisted squeeze names (BBBY, EXPR) can't be back-tested with free price data — survivorship acknowledged.
  • FINRA short interest is twice-monthly with a ~9-business-day publication lag (this evaluation uses only published data); a desk feed (daily borrow/utilization) would sharpen the crowding leg materially.
  • The flag says reduce/size-down risk, not "the top is in" — flags can arrive days to weeks before a peak, but the position is often already dangerous well before the flag fires.
