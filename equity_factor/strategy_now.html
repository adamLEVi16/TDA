The live book carries roughly a quarter of the market's directional risk and a third of its daily volatility — which is what the model said it would do before any money moved. That is the first real out-of-sample confirmation of anything here.

Alpha, however, is not measurable yet — and no broker screen will change that. Alpha is the intercept of a regression; a regression needs a cloud of points to fit a line through. One month is one point. The month's arithmetic reads: beta 0.27 × SPY's −0.57% predicts −0.15%, the book delivered +0.28%, so the residual is +0.43%. Encouraging sign, correct direction, zero statistical weight — the daily residual's t-statistic is 1.01, comfortably inside noise. Roughly 24–36 months is where that number starts to mean something.

The rule, in full

1

At each month-end close, take the eight asset-class ETFs and keep only those trading above their own 10-month simple moving average.

2

Size every position at 1/(trailing 12-month volatility), normalised across all eight assets — so a sleeve that fails the trend test releases its weight to cash rather than to its neighbours.

3

Park the remainder in T-bills (SGOV in the live account), and hold everything unchanged for the following month.

4

Repeat. No discretion, no intra-month decisions, no exceptions — that is what makes the forward test worth anything.

Costs modelled at 10bps per side on turnover. Execution timing is not delicate: the backtest survives rebalancing up to 21 sessions late.

What would break it — and what it isn't

  • It does not beat SPY in dollars. Over the 8-ETF era $100k became $222k here versus $587k in the index. The case is risk-adjusted return and crash resistance, not wealth maximisation.
  • It is behaviourally brutal. It lags buy-and-hold in 69% of rolling three-year windows, once for thirteen straight years. That discomfort is most likely why the premium survives publication.
  • Two-thirds of its retreats to cash are false alarms, costing about 4.5 points each. The edge lives in the third that catch real bears.
  • Fast crashes and V-shaped recoveries are its blind spots — monthly signals cannot dodge a one-week collapse, and it re-enters late (2020).
  • The honest counter-hypothesis: part of the 1982–2021 premium may simply have been a forty-year bond bull market. The 2017–24 decade alpha was roughly flat.
  • Levering it is not free money. At 2.5× with financing at T-bill +100bps, CAGR reaches ~7.5% but Sharpe falls from 0.86 to 0.61 and drawdown triples. Matching SPY's dollars would take ~4.4× and a −39% drawdown.

What would make this a null — committed in advance

Written now, while the first month happens to look good, so it cannot be rewritten later to protect the result. Any one of these is a genuine problem:

Note what is not on this list: losing to SPY. It is expected to lag in 69% of rolling three-year windows — that is the documented cost of the insurance, not evidence against it. A criterion that a strategy fails on its own designed behaviour is not a criterion, it is an excuse to quit.
