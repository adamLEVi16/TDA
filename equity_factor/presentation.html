Multi-Asset Trend + Risk-Parity Strategy

Honest backtest, 2007-03-31 → 2024-12-31 (214 months). Goal: beat buy-and-hold on a risk-adjusted basis — i.e. SPY-like growth with far smaller drawdowns.

The one-line result: Sharpe 0.86 vs SPY 0.71 and 60/40 0.84, with a max drawdown of −6.5% vs SPY's −50.8% — survivorship-free (real ETFs). But read the regime chart: the edge is crash protection, not bull-market alpha.

Cash (when an asset is in a downtrend, that sleeve moves here) earns the 1-month US T-bill yield.

2. The rules (no discretion, no look-ahead)

  1. Trend filter. At each month-end, an asset is held only if its price is above its 10-month moving average; otherwise that sleeve goes to cash (T-bills).
  2. Risk-parity sizing. Held assets are weighted by inverse volatility (trailing 12 months), so calm assets get more, jumpy assets less — no single asset dominates risk.
  3. Monthly rebalance, 10 bps/side transaction costs. Decisions use only data up to the decision date; returns are earned the following month.

Why the control matters. "RP no-trend" holds the same 8 assets by inverse-vol but skips the trend filter: Sharpe 0.58, −20.7% DD. Going from 0.58 → 0.86 and −20.7% → −6.5% is the trend rule earning its keep — the result isn't just "own 8 things".

4. What it actually does over time

In 2008 and 2022 the strategy rotated almost entirely to cash/bonds/gold — that is the mechanism behind the shallow drawdowns.

5. The honest caveat — when it wins, and when it doesn't

Be clear-eyed: the strategy's edge over SPY is concentrated in the 2008 crash. In the 2010–2024 and 2015–2024 bull markets, plain SPY beat it on both Sharpe and return. This is insurance: you trade some bull-market upside for large crash protection. It is the right choice if you care about drawdowns, not if you want to out-return SPY in a calm decade.
